04Design thinking

Why it looks like a lens.

A watch says a lot in a small, round space. Iris works the same way, borrowing from a camera lens and a watch. Four numbers explain most of it.

10Steps

Volume, in detents

Hover the dial and it stretches into a scale with the level at its end.

7Speeds · one move

Any speed in one click

Seven round keys roll out of the speed dial. Not a button pressed six times.

0Repeats

Settings, straight to quality

Rows appear only when there is a real choice. Nothing repeats a key already on screen.

25States

Every state, designed

Loading, errors, captions, phones. Dark frosted surfaces keep each one readable.

06Accessibility

Made for everyone, from the start.

Iris covers every point in the challenge’s accessibility guide, and an automated WCAG 2.1 AA check finds nothing to fix.

BrightDark

Readable over any video

Text and icons sit on dark frosted surfaces, never straight on the picture. They read the same over a bright sky and a black frame. Drag to compare.

Focus you can see

Press Tab and a white ring with a dark halo marks your place on any frame. A tap or click leaves no ring behind.

TabMoveSpacePlay←→Skip↑↓Volume MMuteCCaptionsFFull screenEscClose

Every control by keyboard

Tab reaches every control in the order you see them. Arrow keys move inside menus and the speed keys, and Esc closes them.

Big enough for a thumb

On touch screens every button and the timeline get a touch area of about 44 px, shown dashed here, without changing how they look.

Calm on request

With reduced motion on, rings stop sweeping, the lens blades stay still and the loading ring pulses instead of ticking. Changes fade instead. Try the switch.

Captions that never collide

Sound captions timed to the film. They lift above the controls whenever the controls are on screen.

What a screen reader hears

  1. sliderSeek
  2. buttonPlay
  3. buttonMute
  4. buttonPlayback speed, 1×
  5. buttonEnable captions
  6. buttonSettings
  7. buttonEnter fullscreen
  8. statusPaused

Named for screen readers

Every control has a name and a state, and changes like pausing or muting are announced.

  • 0violations in axe (WCAG 2.1 AA) at desktop and phone sizes, with menus open
  • HCWindows high contrast: the player switches to system colors
  • RTReduced transparency: every surface turns solid

Checked, not assumed

Tested with axe, with the keyboard alone and with touch at phone size, in Chrome and WebKit.

07Install

Add Iris to any page.

Three steps and no build tools. Or take the source and change anything.

  1. 1

    Load Iris

    <script type="module" src="https://iris-player.netlify.app/iris.js"></script>
  2. 2

    Wrap your video

    <media-i18n>
  <video-player>
    <iris-skin>
      <mux-video src="https://stream.mux.com/PLAYBACK_ID.m3u8"
                 playsinline crossorigin></mux-video>
    </iris-skin>
  </video-player>
</media-i18n>

    Any Video.js media element works, including a plain <video>.

  3. 3

    Set your look

    iris-skin {
  --media-accent-color: #f6f2ea;
  --media-border-radius: 16px;
  --media-scale-unit: 16px;
}

    The color and radius follow what you picked in Fits any site.

Own the source Copy the skin into your project Iris is plain HTML and CSS in the Video.js skin format. Start a project with npx @videojs/cli skin create, paste it in and change anything.
